Most of what we use in our research is open source, so you can read how a result was made, run it yourself and build on it. Nearly all of it is built on [GeoDMS](https://www.geodms.nl/), an open-source platform for large spatial models developed by [Object Vision](https://www.objectvision.nl/).

For GIS software and spatial data in general, see [support for researchers](/support/).

## Models

- [Land Use Scanner](/tools/land-use-scanner/) simulates future land use. Its current version, RuimteScanner 2.0, is open source as [RSopen](https://www.geodms.nl/rsopen/).
- [CRISP](https://www.geodms.nl/crisp/) downscales population and built-up area to a 1 km grid worldwide. It produced the [localised projections in the UN's World Urbanization Prospects 2025](/news/world-urbanization-prospects/).

## Tools for Dutch spatial data

- [BAG toolkit](/tools/bag-toolkit/) turns the national register of buildings and addresses into data you can model with, and geocodes your own address files.
- [Pand Hoogte Nederland](https://github.com/ObjectVision/PandHoogteNederland/wiki) gives every BAG building a height, derived from the AHN elevation data.

## Learn how it works

- [Land Use Scanner, student edition](/tools/land-use-scanner/student-edition/) is a working land-use model with its data, to experiment with.
- [GeoDMS Academy](https://www.geodms.nl/academy/) is a course in modules, from installing GeoDMS to starting your own project.
