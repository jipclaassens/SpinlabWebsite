The analysis and simulation of land-use change has long been a central topic at SPINlab. We look at what has driven land-use change in the past, simulate how land use may develop, and assess what that means for policy: for open space, urbanisation and flood risk, among other things.

## Current work

- [RuimteScanner 2.0](https://www.pbl.nl/publicaties/ruimtescanner-20), the current version of the [Land Use Scanner](/tools/land-use-scanner/), simulates land use, actors and objects for the Netherlands. It is [open source as RSopen](https://www.geodms.nl/rsopen/).
- [2UP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2022.101935), an integrated global model of local urban development and population change, and [CRISP](https://www.geodms.nl/crisp/), which downscales population and built-up area to a 1 km grid worldwide. CRISP produced the [localised projections in the UN's World Urbanization Prospects 2025](/news/world-urbanization-prospects/).
- [Classifying urban expansion processes](/news/classifying-urban-expansion-processes/), a new framework that describes the different ways in which cities expand.
- [Possible urbanisation and vulnerable areas](/news/vulnerable-areas-prone-to-urban-development/) in the Netherlands up to 2050.

The models are built on [GeoDMS](https://www.geodms.nl/), developed by [Object Vision](https://www.objectvision.nl/). There is also a [student edition](/tools/land-use-scanner/student-edition/) for teaching.
