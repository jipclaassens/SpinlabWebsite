Spatial planning tries to steer spatial development so that society and the environment benefit, while also meeting other objectives such as economic development, water management and biodiversity. Reconciling these calls for clear information on current developments, insight into possible future trends, and an understanding of what alternative policies would do. SPINlab provides this by combining spatial analysis with newly available, highly detailed data.

## Current work

- [Vulnerable areas prone to urban development](/news/vulnerable-areas-prone-to-urban-development/): around 40% of future urban development in the Netherlands is likely to take place within vulnerable areas.
- [Where to build our houses?](/news/where-to-build-our-houses/): suitable places for housing if soil and water are taken seriously, made for Follow the Money.
- [Richtingen & Ambities Noord-Holland](https://hdl.handle.net/1871.1/a3aa6205-c755-435e-ac2d-172ee3e86d7e): choices for the province mapped with the RuimteScanner (2025).
- Space for [employment](https://hdl.handle.net/1871.1/c8d9e98a-42b0-4e91-820b-6b2ff2ad9bc2) and [recreation](https://hdl.handle.net/1871.1/59c2afcf-886a-4104-b03e-bf934f886ecb): trends and possible developments in the land these uses take up.
- How housing gets built: [Uncovering Dutch residential development processes](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/uncovering-dutch-residential-development-processes/) and [the unforeseen impacts of Dutch devolution on urban density](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0240738).
